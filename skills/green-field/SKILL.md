# Green Field

A thought exercise. Being head down in a problem hides other ways of seeing it, so put the whole
context down and look again.

1. **Find the need.** Say in one sentence what someone ultimately wants, using no words from the
   current solution.
2. **Clear the field.** In one line, name what you are setting aside: the solution, the approach,
   and every constraint, including the ones called mandatory, fixed or required. Do not bring any
   of them back.
3. **Sketch widely first.** Write at least four answers, two or three sentences each, before
   developing any of them. Make them different in kind rather than variations on one idea. Include
   one where the need never arises, and one that would sound odd to someone close to the problem.
4. **Develop one or two.** Take the sketches that open up the most and describe what life looks
   like with them in place.
5. **End on what it opens up.** Close with what you can see now that was hidden before, as an
   observation or a question. Then stop.

Work from imagination, with no tool calls or research. Sketches are not options awaiting a
decision, so do not rank, score or recommend them. Leave out feasibility, cost, migration,
comparison with today, and next steps. Those belong to a later conversation if the user wants one.
